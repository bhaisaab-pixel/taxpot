How the number is worked out

A payment is never taxed on its own, because UK income tax is progressive — the same £1,000 is worth £1,000 to someone who has earned nothing yet this year and £510 to someone already in the higher-rate band. So TaxPot works out your whole-year liability twice and takes the difference:

split(payment) = liability(profit + payment) − liability(profit)

  • Profit is your logged income minus your expenses (or minus the £1,000 trading allowance, whichever you have chosen).
  • Income tax is worked out on your salary plus your profit, then your salary’s own tax is subtracted — which puts the side income on top, where HMRC puts it.
  • Class 4 National Insurance applies to profit only, not to your salary.
  • Pension contributions reduce income tax but not Class 4 NI.
  • Student loan is charged on total income above your plan’s threshold, also as a difference so a salary already over the threshold is not counted twice.
  • The personal allowance tapers away by £1 for every £2 of income over £100,000.
